How to make an invoice

Fill in your business details, your client's details, and what you are charging for. The preview updates as you type. When it looks right, hit Print and save it as a PDF, then email it to your client. The whole thing takes about a minute, and nothing you type leaves your browser.

What every invoice should include

  • Your business name and contact info, so the client knows who is billing them.
  • The client's name and address.
  • A unique invoice number — start at 1001 and count up; it keeps your records clean.
  • Issue date and due date. Net 30 (payment within 30 days) is the standard for most service work.
  • An itemized list of services or products, with quantities and rates. Vague invoices get paid last.
  • The total due, including any tax or discount, stated plainly.
  • Payment instructions — how and where to send the money.

Getting paid faster

Send the invoice the day the work is done, not at the end of the month. Keep due dates specific ("due November 14") instead of vague. And when clients start paying you regularly, a payments platform like the ones above adds automatic reminders and one-click payment, which is where late payments go to die.

Frequently asked questions

Is this invoice generator really free?

Yes. No account, no trial, no watermark on your invoice, and no limit on how many you make.

How do I save my invoice as a PDF?

Click "Print / Save as PDF" and choose "Save as PDF" as the printer destination. Only the invoice itself prints — the form and the rest of the page stay out of it.

Is my information stored anywhere?

No. Everything runs in your browser. Your business details, client details, and amounts are never uploaded to a server.

What is a good invoice numbering scheme?

Start at 1001 and increase by one for every invoice. Simple, sequential, and easy to search later.

When should payment be due?

Net 30 is the most common, but many freelancers use Net 14 or Net 7 to get paid faster. Whatever you choose, put the actual calendar date on the invoice.
